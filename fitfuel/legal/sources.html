Sources and methods

Where FitFuel's numbers come from. All of it is general information, not medical advice (Health disclaimer).

Food and nutrient data

  • USDA FoodData Central (U.S. Department of Agriculture, Agricultural Research Service), fdc.nal.usda.gov: generic foods from SR Legacy (with some mixed dishes from the FNDDS survey database and a few Foundation Foods values), and packaged foods with barcodes from the USDA Global Branded Food Products Database (label data supplied by manufacturers), including household portions. USDA data is in the public domain (CC0). The data ships inside the app; FitFuel does not contact the USDA while you use it. How the data was checked is described in the project's validation report.
  • Brand and restaurant items built into the app (about 2,200) were compiled from manufacturers' labels and restaurants' published nutrition information. They are per-serving approximations, could not be checked against USDA, and recipes change: check the label or the restaurant's own information when it matters. Brand and restaurant names belong to their owners; FitFuel is not affiliated with or endorsed by any of them.
  • Foods, meals and labels you enter yourself are stored exactly as you typed them.
  • Energy per gram (Atwater general factors): protein 4 kcal, carbohydrate 4, fat 9, alcohol 7; fiber is counted at 2 kcal in the TEF calculator.

Energy needs and targets (Food tab)

  • Resting energy: Mifflin-St Jeor equation. Mifflin MD, St Jeor ST, et al. A new predictive equation for resting energy expenditure in healthy individuals. Am J Clin Nutr. 1990;51(2):241-247.
  • Activity multipliers (1.2 to 1.9) chosen from your daily steps: conventional physical-activity-level factors used with resting-energy equations.
  • Ages 13 to 17: Estimated Energy Requirement equations from the Institute of Medicine, Dietary Reference Intakes for Energy, Carbohydrate, Fiber, Fat, Fatty Acids, Cholesterol, Protein, and Amino Acids (2005). Under 18, FitFuel sets targets to maintenance only.
  • Weight-loss target: a deficit of at most 500 kcal a day or 20% of estimated expenditure, whichever is smaller, never set below 1,500 kcal (men) or 1,200 kcal (everyone else) by the app. Weight-gain target: about 300 kcal above expenditure.
  • Energy in body-weight change: about 3,500 kcal per pound, the common rule of thumb (Wishnofsky M. Am J Clin Nutr. 1958;6(5):542-546). It is an approximation; real changes vary (Hall KD, et al. Lancet. 2011;378:826-837).
  • Adaptive expenditure: FitFuel's own running estimate from your logged calories and the trend of your weigh-ins. It is only as good as your logging.
  • Vitamin and mineral targets: Dietary Reference Intakes (RDA/AI and upper limits) by age and sex, National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine.
  • Macro targets: protein is set at about 1 to 1.1 g per pound of body weight (at most 45% of calories), fat at roughly 27 to 30% of calories, and carbohydrate fills the rest. These are common planning figures for active people, above the minimum protein RDA of 0.8 g per kg; you can change them.

Body Comp tab

  • Maintenance from lean mass: Katch-McArdle equation (370 + 21.6 × fat-free mass in kg), times an activity factor, plus workout calories.
  • Rates of muscle gain by training age and the energy cost of new tissue are rough, widely used planning figures, shown on the page as adjustable "Model assumptions". Body-fat and scan measurements have their own error. Treat every projection as a rough guide.

TEF tab

  • Thermic effect of food: protein 20 to 30% of its calories, carbohydrate 5 to 10%, fat 0 to 3%, alcohol 10 to 30%. Westerterp KR. Diet induced thermogenesis. Nutr Metab (Lond). 2004;1:5.

Workouts tab

  • Sets per muscle: each working set counts 1 for an exercise's primary muscles and ½ for secondary muscles. This is a bookkeeping convention, not a measurement.
  • No load suggestions: the Workouts tab shows what you did last time and never suggests a weight, a rep count or a one-rep max. Choose loads that are safe for you.
