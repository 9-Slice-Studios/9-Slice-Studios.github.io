How it works: maintenance = Katch-McArdle BMR (from fat-free mass) × daily activity + workout calories. Your ceiling for lean gain comes from training age (≈1–1.5% of bodyweight/month for beginners, 0.5–1% intermediate, 0.25–0.5% advanced, under a surplus). A recomp keeps your weight the same while it becomes more muscle and less fat. Without a surplus that ceiling is scaled down by the recomp rate, then by training frequency, protein (full credit at 0.8 g/lb) and sleep. Every lb of muscle gained replaces a lb of fat, so the scale does not move. Fat holds more energy than muscle costs to build, so the intake that holds your weight is slightly under maintenance. It's an estimate, not a promise: real-world numbers vary a lot, and DEXA error (~1–2 lb lean) can be bigger than a few months of recomp. Re-scan under the same conditions to check.

How it works: your goal is turned into the lean and fat mass you need to add or lose. The planner then tries each way of getting there — bulk then cut, cut then bulk, and recomp then cut — using the same models as the other tabs at the phase speeds you set, and picks the fastest one that hits both targets. In "cut then bulk" it cuts just enough that the fat gained while bulking lands you on your goal. Lean gains slow as you get more advanced, so long timelines are rough estimates; re-scan every few months and update your inputs.

How it works: your lean-gain ceiling comes from training age (≈1–1.5% of bodyweight/month for beginners, 0.5–1% intermediate, 0.25–0.5% advanced), scaled by training frequency, protein, sleep and how big the surplus is (full speed at ~+300 kcal/day). Surplus energy that goes into building muscle (~2,500 kcal per lb) is used up; whatever's left is stored as fat at 3,500 kcal per lb. So past the point your muscle can use, a bigger surplus mostly adds fat. Maintenance is re-estimated each week from your new fat-free mass. Early scale jumps from water and glycogen (often 1–3 lb) aren't shown.

How it works: each week, maintenance is re-estimated from your new fat-free mass (Katch-McArdle × activity + workouts), so a fixed intake gives a shrinking deficit. Fat loss = your weekly deficit (plus the energy spent building any new muscle) ÷ kcal per lb of fat. Training, protein and sleep still drive a little muscle growth in a modest deficit, using the same model as the Recomp tab. If you expect some muscle loss, set a lean share under Model assumptions. Water and glycogen shifts (often 2–4 lb early in a cut) aren't shown.
